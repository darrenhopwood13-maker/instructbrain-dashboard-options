Dashboard design options - 11 Oct 2026

Three directions for the dashboard.

Same data, three different beliefs about what a dashboard is for. They are not colour variations - each one is a separate argument. All three are clickable: hover the charts, click a row, drag across the little squares, switch the grouping.

On a phone: the tab shows a desktop screen shrunk to fit, so use Open on its own for the full thing. All three are built with the product's own colours, so what you see is what the real screen can be - not a picture of a different product.

1. Control Room Everything on one screen, readouts and a live instrument. Open on its own

Every variant is a single self-contained page with no build step, so it will open on any phone or laptop.

What I would do

Lead with 3, keep 2 underneath it.

The grid is the one people have not seen. It answers a question your competitors' dashboards cannot - where the open items actually are, and how many nobody has even looked at - and the drag-select turns it from a picture into a tool, because you pull a set out and build a document from them. That is the moment someone says "I have not seen that before".

  • The bar it has to clear: a director must understand it without being taught. The legend and the "needs attention now" list underneath are what make that work.
  • Why keep the Ledger too: it is the one you hand to a client or put in a meeting pack. It needs no explanation at all. Cheap to build once the numbers exist.
  • My honest read on 1: it looks the most impressive in a screenshot, but the density hurts on a phone and it hides the unassessed items. It is the best look, not the best tool.
The one thing that shapes all three (and it is not visual)

I checked the live data before designing anything. Every item in the account is from October 2026. The product is about a month old, so a six-month trend chart draws one spike and five flat months - it looks broken while being perfectly truthful.

That is why variant 2 says out loud that the line is short by design, and why the grid earns its place: with a month of data, a map reads as full, while a trend line reads as empty. When you demo to a prospect with two reports on their account, the map will still look alive and a chart will not.

It also means every one of these needs a real empty state - a brand new account has zero of everything, and that is the state a prospect sees first.

What is real here, and what is not

These are design mockups, not the product. The figures are sample data, shaped from your real records: the project names, the reference formats (DEMO-001, HCE-2417, EIC-2026-10-001) and the survey types are lifted from the live database. The item counts and states are illustrative.

Nothing has been published to the product, and nothing will be without you saying so first. The colours are the product's own tokens - navy #24417B and Laser Green #57FF00 - so a chosen direction can be built with the existing design system rather than bolted on.
